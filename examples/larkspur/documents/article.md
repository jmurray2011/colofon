# The first twenty minutes

Human vision does not switch instantly from a bright room to a dark observing deck. A field
team that arrives under white light will miss faint details and may describe contrast
differently from a team that has adapted gradually.

At Larkspur, observers begin with dim red task lights, lower screen brightness, and postpone
fine visual estimates until the opening checklist is complete. The pause is not idle time:
it is used to confirm the target, compare clocks, and agree on vocabulary.

# Notes that another observer can use

Strong notes distinguish what was seen from what was inferred. Record the time, sky
condition, instrument, and eyepiece before interpretation. Prefer a comparison such as
"fainter than the western reference star" to a vague adjective such as "dim."

> [!tip]
> Write the observation first. Add an explanation only after the direct description is
> complete.

# A repeatable habit

Dark adaptation is valuable because it makes observations more comparable. A short,
consistent arrival routine reduces one source of variation and leaves the team free to
focus on the sky.
